Retirement planning

Retirement is the only goal you cannot borrow for. There is no loan for being seventy with too little money, which is exactly why it loses every year to goals that shout louder.

The work is simple to describe and uncomfortable to do: calculate what your lifestyle will cost when you stop earning, calculate what your current pace builds, and look at the difference while you still have years to act on it.

The four numbers that decide your retirement

Everything else is detail. Get these wrong and no fund selection rescues the plan.

Your real expenses

Not your salary — what you actually spend, minus the costs that end (EMIs, children, commuting) and plus the ones that grow (healthcare, help at home).

Inflation

At 6% a year, costs roughly triple over twenty years. Medical inflation in India has historically run higher than general inflation, and that is the bill that arrives last.

How long it must last

Retire at 58 and plan to 85 and the corpus has to fund twenty-seven years without a salary — longer than many people spend working.

Withdrawal rate

What you can draw each year without running out. Get this wrong and a bad first decade of markets does damage that later good years cannot undo.

What we do

  • Cost your retirement in today’s money, then inflate it to your actual retirement year
  • Count what already exists — EPF, PPF, NPS, gratuity, existing investments, rental income, and what each will realistically be worth
  • Project your current pace and show you where it lands against the target
  • Size the gap and convert it into a monthly figure you either can or cannot afford — we will tell you which
  • Choose the vehicles by asset class and tax treatment: what belongs in EPF and NPS, what belongs in market investments, what should stay liquid
  • Plan the drawdown — the order to sell from, how much to keep in cash, and how to avoid selling equity in a bad year
  • Cover the health bill — the part most retirement plans ignore until premiums become unaffordable at the age you need them

If the honest answer is that your target retirement age does not work, we will say so early, while the options are still cheap: retire later, spend less, save more, or earn for longer in a lighter job. Finding out at 55 is much worse than finding out at 40.

Common questions

Is EPF and NPS enough on its own?

For most people with a middle-class or higher lifestyle, no. EPF and NPS form a solid base, but they are usually sized for a fraction of the spending people expect to continue. The plan is to know the fraction rather than to assume it is all handled.

I am 45 and have started late. Is it too late?

It is late, not too late, and the honest version matters: you have fewer compounding years, so the monthly figure is larger and the choices are starker. Most people in this position close more of the gap than they expect, largely by raising their savings rate rather than by chasing returns.

Should I buy an annuity?

Sometimes, for part of the corpus, to cover essential expenses with income that cannot run out. Annuity rates in India are modest and the income is taxable, so it is a trade-off between certainty and growth. We size the portion rather than treat it as all or nothing — and we do not earn anything on what you buy.

Can I retire early?

Maybe. Early retirement means a bigger corpus and a longer drawdown, so the maths gets harder in both directions at once, and you lose employer health cover for decades. We will run it properly rather than talk you into or out of it.
