Personal financial planning, written down

A comprehensive financial plan is not a fund recommendation. It is the document that answers four questions: where your money currently goes, what you are trying to buy with your life, whether the gap between those two is closeable, and what to do on Monday morning.

What a VRPF financial plan covers

Every engagement covers all eight. We do not sell these separately, because they do not work separately — a tax decision changes a cash-flow decision, which changes a retirement date.

Cash flow and savings rate

Where the money goes each month, and the honest number you can save without hating your life. The savings rate is the single biggest lever in any plan — bigger than fund selection, by a distance.

Goals, costed and dated

A house in 2031, a child’s education in 2038, retirement at 58. Each one gets a rupee figure adjusted for inflation and a monthly contribution that actually reaches it.

Emergency fund and liquidity

How many months of expenses you can cover without selling something at a bad time, and where that money should sit so it is boring and available.

Asset allocation

The split between equity, debt, and cash or gold that suits your risk profile and time horizon — expressed as ranges, with what to add and what to trim to get there.

Insurance needs analysis

How much term cover your family actually needs if your income stops, how much health cover is realistic now, and which of your existing policies are investments wearing a costume.

Debt strategy

Which loan to kill first, which to let run, and whether prepaying your home loan beats investing the same rupee. The answer is not always the one that feels better.

Tax-aware structuring

Old regime against new for your numbers, how your RSUs and capital gains are taxed, and how to use EPF, NPS and PPF sensibly rather than in a March panic.

Retirement projection

The corpus your lifestyle requires, what your current pace actually builds, and the size of the gap while there is still time to close it. More on retirement planning →

What is deliberately not included

Being clear about the edges of the service is part of being honest about it.

  • No specific stock or scheme recommendations. We work at the level of asset classes and categories. Security-specific advice in India requires SEBI registration as an Investment Adviser, which we do not hold.
  • No handling of your money. We never take custody, never ask for passwords, and never place a transaction for you. Your money stays where only you can move it.
  • No product sales. We are not distributors. If a plan calls for term insurance, we tell you the cover amount and the type — you buy it wherever you like, and we earn nothing either way.
  • No return promises. Nobody can make them honestly. Our projections are clearly labelled assumptions, and we show you what happens when they are wrong.

If what you need is security-level advice or discretionary management, say so on the call. We will point you to a SEBI-registered adviser rather than stretch our remit.
