About financial planning generally

What does a financial planner actually do?

A financial planner looks at your whole financial life rather than one product. That means your income and spending, everything you own and owe, your insurance, your tax position, and the goals you are trying to fund — then produces a written plan that says what to do, in what order, with what money.

It is a different job from choosing investments. Investment selection is a small part of a plan and rarely the part that decides whether you reach your goals. Your savings rate, your insurance and your debt decisions usually matter more.

What is the difference between a financial planner and a SEBI-registered investment adviser?

In India, giving advice on specific securities — which stock, which mutual fund scheme — for consideration requires registration with SEBI as an Investment Adviser (RIA). RIAs are regulated, must act as fiduciaries, and must follow SEBI's rules on fees, disclosure and complaint handling.

Financial planning that stops short of security-specific recommendations — budgeting, goal planning, savings rate, asset-class allocation, insurance needs, retirement projections — is what we do. VRPF is not a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser. If you want someone to tell you which specific scheme to buy, you should use an RIA, and we will help you find one. Our full disclaimer →

How do I know if an adviser has a conflict of interest?

Ask one question: who pays you, and how much, for each recommendation you make? If any part of the answer involves the manufacturer of a product — commission, trail, brokerage, referral fee — then an incentive exists. That does not make the advice wrong, but you should know it is there.

Do I need a financial planner, or can I do this myself?

Plenty of people do it themselves well. If you have one income, an emergency fund, adequate term insurance, a low-cost diversified portfolio and no complicated moving parts, a few good books and some discipline may be all you need.

Planning tends to earn its fee when there is complexity (equity compensation, a business, cross-border money), when a big irreversible decision is close (a house, a career break, early retirement), or when the plan keeps not happening despite good intentions. We will give you our honest view on the free call, including when it is no.

When is the right time to start?

The two most useful moments are when your income jumps and when your life changes — marriage, a child, a move abroad, a return to India, a new business. Both are points where the default path quietly sets itself for the next decade.

Will you manage my investments for me?

No. We never take custody of your money, never ask for passwords, and never place transactions on your behalf. You keep control and you execute. We are the people who tell you what to execute and why.

Do you recommend specific mutual funds or stocks?

No. We work at the level of asset classes and categories — how much equity, how much debt, how much liquid. Specific security recommendations require SEBI registration as an Investment Adviser, which we do not hold, and we will not blur that line.
