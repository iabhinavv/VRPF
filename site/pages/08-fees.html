The three ways financial advice is paid for in India

It is worth understanding all three, because the payment model shapes the advice far more than most people realise.

ModelWho paysWhat it means for you
Commission / distribution The product manufacturer pays the adviser Advice is free at the point of delivery, and the cost is inside the product. The adviser earns more from some products than others, which is a conflict whether or not it is abused.
Percentage of assets You, as a percentage of the money managed Transparent, but the bill rises with your portfolio even when the work does not, and the adviser is not neutral about advice like “pay off your home loan”.
Flat fee — what we do You, once, for the work You can see the price. It does not change with the size of your portfolio, and we have no stake in which products you choose or whether you invest at all.

We have never earned a rupee of commission. We are not distributors and not registered as such. If your plan says you need term insurance, we tell you the cover amount and type — you buy it wherever you like, and we earn exactly nothing either way.

What drives the number

We quote on the first call, once we know what your situation actually involves. The fee depends on the work, not on your net worth:

  • How many moving parts — one salary and three funds is a smaller job than two incomes, equity compensation, a business and property in two cities
  • Cross-border complexity — NRI situations take longer because accounts, residency rules and timing all interact
  • Whether a business is involved — separating business and household finances adds a full workstream
  • Ongoing support — a one-time plan costs less than a plan with quarterly reviews and a dashboard kept current all year

You get the number, the scope and what is excluded in writing before you pay anything. There is no upsell at the end of the plan, because there is nothing else for us to sell you.

Is paying for financial planning worth it?

Sometimes it genuinely is not, and we would rather tell you that on a free call than take a fee for work you do not need. If your finances are one salary, an emergency fund, a term policy and an index fund, you may be better served by reading for a weekend than by hiring anyone.

It tends to be worth it when the cost of the common mistakes is larger than the fee. In practice that means: a wrong insurance product running for a decade, a portfolio concentrated in one employer’s stock, a tax regime choice made without the numbers, an emergency fund that does not exist when the emergency does, or a retirement gap discovered at 55 instead of 40.

Those are not exotic situations. They are what we find in most plans we open.

A word on comparison. If you are speaking to other firms, ask each one a single question: who pays you, and how much, for every recommendation you make? The answer tells you more than any brochure.

Questions about money and fees

Why isn’t the price on the website?

Because the work genuinely varies, and a single number on a page would be wrong for most people who read it. You get a firm quote on the first call, before any commitment, and the call itself is free.

Do you charge a percentage of my investments?

No. The fee is flat. Someone with ₹20 lakh and someone with ₹2 crore in a similar situation pay similar amounts, because the work is similar.

What if I don’t think it was worth it?

Tell us within 14 days of your plan walkthrough and we refund the fee in full. We would rather return a fee than keep one we did not earn.

Is there a lock-in or notice period?

No. Ongoing support is renewable, not a contract you have to escape from.
